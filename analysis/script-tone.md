# Script tone guide: Talha's voice, sharpened by reference creators

**References:** @aidendjohnston (9 reels). Add more with `.claude/skills/script-agent/SKILL.md` §6. **Approved example of Talha's style:** `scripts/2026-11-23-jensen-is-back.md` (v3).

Source: 9 @aidendjohnston reels transcribed on 23 Nov 2026 (`analysis/creators/aidendjohnston/scripts/`, private, gitignored), merged with Talha's own scripts in `scripts/`. **Patterns and rules only.** Never reuse the other creator's lines, jokes, slang signatures ("LARPer", "Peace") or identity. The aim is **Talha writing in that rhythm**.

## 0. Talha's rule #1 (2026-11-23, outranks everything below)
**Conversational and Gen Z.** It should sound like he's talking to a mate, not presenting. Hooks often **ask the viewer a question built on a Gen Z concept, then pivot to the story**: "Do you want to know the definition of aura? Then let me tell you about [person]." Gen Z words are welcome when they fit: aura, cooked, W / L, crazy, wild, mad, no cap, "he's him", "the audacity", "chat, is this real?". He mixes them with his British/Manchester words. Use 2–4 per reel, never forced.

**Rule #2 (2026-11-23): no comma lists, no rule-of-three.** Never write "X, Y, the lot" or "A, B and C" triplets. Max **one comma per line**, and most lines have none. If a line needs commas, split it into two sentences or say it the way he'd say it out loud. Triplets and comma chains read as AI slop.

**Rule #3 (2026-11-23): flow like real talk, no fragments.** No one- or two-word "sentences" ("Affairs. A spy story." "Eleven days. That's it." "Unanimously."). No full-stop drumbeats. Write **full sentences that run on naturally** the way he speaks, linked with *and, so, but, because, like, and then*. Rules #2 and #3 together: not comma lists and not choppy stops, just **one person talking in a flowing line**. Use his own posted scripts in `scripts/` as the model for sentence length (often 15–30 words).

## 2. How the first two lines work
**Line 1 is a hook aimed at "you", or a bold claim.** Five shapes they use (fill them with Talha's own words):
1. **The accusation:** "If you [do a normal thing], you're [losing out] unless [twist]."
2. **The comparison:** "Ever wondered why [someone else] gets [result] while yours [worse result]?"
3. **The "you've seen it" call-out:** "You ever see [ridiculous claim] and think, how is that even possible?"
4. **The famous-name pivot:** "Everyone knows [famous person/story]. Not many people know [the hidden part]."
5. **The confession:** "I [did/am doing something extreme]."
6. **The slang-question pivot (Talha's own, use first):** "Do you want to know the definition of [aura / being cooked / a W]? Then let me tell you about [X]."

**Line 2 names the format and promises the payoff:** "This is [Topic] 101, and by the end of this video you'll [concrete result]". They usually add **a joke twist on the promise** (an absurd side-effect). Talha's version: the promise, then a cheeky British aside.

**Then the thesis in one flowing sentence** (not a three-beat drumbeat; see rule #3).

## 3. Building the middle
- **Credibility early, from experience:** "To prove I'm not chatting rubbish, here's…" plus a real screenshot or number from **his own** use. Talha: his engineering job, his own Claude setup, his own reels.
- **One everyday analogy per reel:** an employee, a shop, a gym membership. Make it British and real (a corner shop, a Greggs, a gym you never go to).
- **Numbered steps** for how-tos ("Number one… Number two…", 3–5 steps). Each step is **one rule + one line of why + one example from his own life**.
- **Every sentence sets up the next:** end a line on a small open question or a contrast, then pay it off ("…but that's not the crazy part." / "And here's where it gets stupid.").
- **Numbers, said and repeated:** give the exact figure, then **repeat it as its own beat** for weight ("Nine publications. Yes, nine."). Use the shape, not the line.
- **A punchline after the serious point:** a deadpan undercut after anything technical, one per 15–20 s, never more.
- **A rant gear:** one "for the love of God" style escalation on a take he really holds. **Only if Talha actually holds the view.** Never invent his opinion.

## 4. Register and how he talks to viewers
- Gen Z register **is wanted** (see rule #1), but in Talha's mix, not the reference creator's signature words. Avoid his signatures ("LARP", "mogs", "Peace").
- **Talha's register (keep):** Manchester and British. "you guys", "boys", "bloody hell", "big doggies", "damn", "that is insane", "sticky spot", "cracked developers like myself". Plain words, no corporate tone.
- Always **"you"**. Talk to one viewer, then "you guys" for the call to action.

## 5. Endings
- **A callback line** that wraps the reel in one image, often the analogy again ("…that's like a gym membership you never use").
- Then the **series tag + follow line**. Theirs is a running "day N until [goal]". **Talha doesn't have a series yet.** If he starts one, the goal is his to choose. Until then: his CTA ("comment WORD and I'll send it to you directly", or "follow for more") **plus his sign-off "Let's go, boys."**
- Optional **last-second joke** after the sign-off (a deadpan tag). Keep it to one line.

## 6. Do / don't
**Do**
- Hook in line 1, format + promise in line 2, thesis by line 5 (~8 s).
- One analogy, one number repeated, one punchline per section.
- Steps and evidence from Talha's own life (engineer, Claude user, creator).
- Mark rounded or unverified claims; the scout's fact check travels with the script.
- **Verdict lines stay as `[VERDICT: your words]` placeholders.**

**Don't**
- Copy any line, joke, nickname or slang signature from the source creator.
- Invent Talha's opinion, rants or personal experiences. Use placeholders: `[YOUR EXAMPLE: …]`.
- Go over ~280 words.
- Write fragments or drumbeat stops (rule #3), or comma lists (rule #2).
- Copy the reference creator's signature words ("LARP", "mogs", "Peace").
- Sound like a presenter. If a line sounds read out, rewrite it as how he'd say it to a mate.

## 7. How this guide learns
After Talha edits or films a script, the script agent compares the **draft vs what he actually said** (from the transcript of his footage) and logs it in `analysis/script-feedback.md` (lines he cut, words he swapped, hooks he rewrote). When a change repeats 2–3 times, it updates this guide and says what changed.
