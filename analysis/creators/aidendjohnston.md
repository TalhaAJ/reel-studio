## 3. Format
- **Talking head, handheld mic, his real room** (posters behind). No green screen and no fancy edit.
- **Hook = a white title box at the top** in sentence case ("Why Cluely will be a BILLION dollar company", "AI Agents 101", "the stats that got me rejected from 13 schools"): the topic in ≤8 words, readable before the sound comes on.
- **Burned-in captions**, white caps, 2–4 words, mid-frame (the same Instagram-native style you chose).
- **Proof cards** pop up over him: a laptop clip, a ChatGPT answer, a college letter.
- **The interview series** has a different look: black band on top with key words in **yellow** ("$140K", "21-year-olds"), split-screen call.
- Thumbnail is consistent: "Day N" in huge white letters.

## 4. Comments (why they're big)
- People **argue**: "All this to found a shitty AI app 💀" (590 likes), "d1 glazer" (1k likes) under the Cluely reel; "$100k revenue isn't profit"-type pushback.
- People **ask practical questions**: "how much did it cost to run it in a loop?", "T3 Code, Claude Code or Hermes, which one?", "why a Mac mini instead of a sandbox?". Each one is a free follow-up video idea.
- One-word **CTA comments** ("REEL") inflate the comment count, so a comment count isn't proof of interest on its own.

## 5. What to take for your channel
1. **Run a public series with a counter and a goal.** Something true to you, e.g. "Day 1 of explaining one AI thing a day until 10k followers", or "until my AI agent makes £1,000". It gives people a reason to follow you, not just watch one reel. *Your call; never invent the goal for you.*
2. **Add a title box to every hook:** the topic in ≤8 words at the top, alongside your captions.
3. **Lean on the lanes that are his outliers:** famous founder/company stories (you already do Jensen), "X 101" explainers, "here's my agent setup", calling BS on money posts.
4. **Mine the comments for sequels:** the scout lists the top practical questions under each outlier as ready-made next videos.
5. **Show your own stakes now and then:** your engineering background, your rejections, your numbers. That's what turns topic viewers into followers.
