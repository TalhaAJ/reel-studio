## 3. How he builds a reel
1. **The opinion comes first.** In 9 of 9 it's inside the first two sentences. The rest of the reel defends it.
2. **The promise is on screen, not spoken.** 9 of 9 open with a black title box (two lines, mostly lower case with a word or two in CAPS) that stays 3.5–7 s and clears on or near a cut. It carries the topic, question or payoff, so his first spoken line can skip straight to the answer. **0 of 9 say "by the end of this video".** Two do frame the format in speech, but only as the hook itself.
3. **Reason, then a ridiculously specific example.** Every claim gets a named thing rather than a category: a certain game and the exact losing streak, a certain show, a certain university.
4. **He acts out someone's inner voice** in 6 of 9, usually with a punch-in on the acted line.
5. **He names the other side, then turns on it.** 5 of 9 have a named opponent group, and 5 of 9 use the same "in reality" pivot.
6. **He gives one thing away.** 4 of 9 admit an exception or the other side's fair point, which makes the rest sound honest.
7. **He lands on the opening claim.** 6 of 9 end by clearly restating it.
8. **Almost no asks.** 0 of 9 say follow. 1 plugs a product, 1 asks for comments, 1 asks people to send it on.

## 6. Take
1. Opinion in the first two sentences.
2. Promise on a title box of 8 words or fewer, never spoken.
3. One acted line per reel.
4. Swap every general noun for a named one.
5. Say what the other side would say, then answer it.
6. One honest concession.
7. Land back on the opening claim.
