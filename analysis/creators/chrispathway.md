## 3. Format
- **Clean, bright desk or home set**, glasses, a handheld mic, a calm "smart friend" delivery.
- **Small white captions**, 1–3 words, centred low (lowercase-ish, understated, not shouty).
- **Topic label at the top in yellow** ("Markets") or a **logo floating behind him** (the Python logo), so the topic reads instantly.
- **Screen and B-roll heavy:** papers, code, the dashboard he built, the gadget on his desk.
- **Caption = the full pitch + CTA:** what it is, the proof number (20,000 images, 99.5%, $30,000/year), then "Comment „WORD" and I'll send you…".

## 4. Comments
- Almost all one-word CTA replies, which tells you **the reel's goal is DMs and follows, not debate**. Engagement here is "I want the thing".

## 5. What to take for your channel
1. **"It costs £X, so I built/found it free"** is a killer frame for your tools lane: Bloomberg Terminal → free, ElevenLabs → VoiceStudio (you already did that one). Lead with the price.
2. **Give a tangible thing every time:** code, a prompt, a roadmap, a repo. Your "comment WORD" CTA works best when the thing is concrete ("the exact prompt", "the dataset").
3. **A proof number in the first line:** "20,000 scans, 99.5%", "$30,000 a year". You already like big sayable numbers.
4. **Career framing for the beginners lane:** "this project got me my first job", "the roadmap I'd follow if I started today". As a software engineer you can do this with real authority.
5. **Organise the profile like a library** (Resources, Roadmaps highlights) so a new follower can binge. That's a profile tip, not a reel tip.
